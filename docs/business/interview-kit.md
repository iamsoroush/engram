# Engram — Customer Discovery & Validation Interview Kit

> **Purpose:** one kit the interviewer uses end-to-end to (1) learn how clinics work today, (2) find and
> size the pains aes-Basic/Pro and therapy are meant to solve, (3) validate that those pains are real and
> worth paying to fix, (4) react to a product demo, and (5) probe willingness-to-pay.
> **Status:** pre-PMF. This is the master (English). The interviewer-facing version is Farsi
> ([handoff version](../../../Documents/wtp-interview-guide-fa.md) covers the WTP block today; a full Farsi
> translation of this kit follows once you approve it).
>
> **Golden rule (read first):** you are here to *learn their world*, not to sell. Talk about their life,
> not our idea. Demo comes **late, on purpose** — pitching early poisons the discovery.

---

## Part 0 — Interviewer principles (The Mom Test, condensed)

1. **Ask about the past, not the future.** "When did this last happen? What did you do?" — not "Would you
   use…?" Hypotheticals and compliments are not data.
2. **Talk about their life, not our product** (until the demo). Their workflow, their last bad day.
3. **Dig for emotion and specifics.** "That sounds frustrating — what happened exactly? How often?"
4. **Chase evidence of pain:** has it cost them money/patients/time? **Have they already tried to fix it,
   or paid for anything?** Spending is the strongest signal.
5. **Seek commitment, not praise.** A good interview advances something real: time (a pilot), reputation
   (an intro), or money. "Sounds great!" with no commitment = a polite no.
6. **You talk ~20%, they talk ~80%.** Silence is your friend.
7. **Capture exact quotes.** One vivid quote beats a paragraph of paraphrase.
8. **Don't lead.** Avoid "Don't you hate when…?" Ask neutral, open questions.

## Part 1 — Interview plan

### 1.1 Objectives of this round
- Map the **current workflow + tools** for the jobs Engram targets (capture, patient memory, photos,
  search, reports, patient questions).
- Identify and **rank pains** by *frequency × severity × what they already spend* on them.
- **Validate / kill** our core hypotheses (Part 5).
- Get **demo reactions** and **WTP** signals, value-anchored.
- Leave each interview with a **commitment** (pilot, intro, or a clear no + why).

### 1.2 Segment coverage (Tehran-only this wave — by design)
We only have Tehran access now, so this wave covers the **size** and **vertical/tier** axes, **not** the
geographic-WTP axis. Keep that bias front of mind (see 1.6).

| # | Segment (Tehran) | Vertical / tier focus | Target interviews |
|---|---|---|--:|
| S1 | Elite / large multi-doctor clinic | aesthetics — Pro / future Enterprise | 3 |
| S2 | Small or solo clinic | aesthetics — Basic + Pro upsell | 3 |
| S3 | Group / multi-therapist practice | therapy — single plan | 2 |
| S4 | Solo practitioner | therapy — single plan | 2 |
| | **First-wave total** | | **~10** |

Within S1/S3 (multi-person), also grab a **10-min side conversation with an assistant/receptionist** — the
capture-first / never-block / multi-role hypotheses live or die at that desk, and the owner won't see it.

### 1.3 Who to talk to (roles — they see different truths)
- **Owner / lead doctor** — buys, feels the business pain (retention, marketing, liability).
- **Practising doctor / therapist** — feels the daily documentation pain.
- **Assistant / receptionist** — feels intake, filing, and the "where is X" pain.
Ideally interview ≥2 roles in the larger clinics.

### 1.4 Recruitment
- Warm network first (faster, forgiving for script-debugging), then cold.
- Aesthetics cold outreach works via **Instagram DMs** (they live there); therapy via professional
  networks / referrals. Offer a small incentive (free pilot seat, a gift) + share findings back.
- Start with **2 friendly "pilot interviews"** to debug the script, then run the rest.

### 1.5 Logistics
- **45–60 min:** ~30 discovery · ~15 demo · ~10 WTP + close.
- **In person if possible** — you want to *see* the Apple Notes mess, the camera roll, the paper file.
  Ask "can you show me on your phone how you'd find patient X?" (observed behavior > reported behavior).
- Two people if you can: one interviews, one notes. **Record with consent.**
- Fill a **capture sheet (Part 4) within 10 min of leaving**, while it's fresh.

### 1.6 The Tehran bias — do not forget
Tehran over-indexes on **willingness-to-pay, tech-savviness, marketing sophistication, and English/app
comfort**. Treat every WTP and adoption finding as **"Tehran-segment only."** Do **not** set national
prices from this wave. Province-center and small-city waves are required before pricing nationally — flag
this explicitly in the readout.

## Part 2 — The interview flow

> Order matters: **context → their workflow → their pain → (problem reflect-back) → demo → WTP → adoption →
> commitment.** Don't reveal the product or prices before the demo section.

### A. Intro & consent (2 min)
- Who you are, that this is research not a sales call, ~45–60 min, honesty is the gift.
- Ask permission to record. Confirm role + that they can show you their actual tools.

### B. Context / firmographics (3 min)
1. City, clinic type (segment S1–S4), vertical.
2. How many doctors/therapists? How many patients/clients per month? Returns per year (for LTV later)?
3. Your role; **who decides on software purchases here?**

### C. Current workflow & tools (10 min — observe, don't lead)
*(Aesthetics)*
4. Walk me through a full patient visit, start to finish: arrival → consult → procedure → photos → notes →
   checkout → follow-up. **What tool/device at each step?** (paper, Apple Notes, camera roll, WhatsApp,
   Excel, a clinic system, Instagram)
5. **Before/after photos:** how do you take them, where are they stored, how are they named, how do you
   find them months later? Consent? Do you share them with the patient?
6. A returning patient walks in — **show me how you'd pull up their history.** How long does it take? Has
   it ever failed at a bad moment?
7. "What product/dose/lot did I use last time?" — how do you answer that today?
8. Between visits, how do patients reach you with questions? Roughly how many a day? Who answers?
9. Multi-doctor: how is a patient's record shared across doctors / assistant / reception?

*(Therapy — swap C with these)*
4T. Walk me through a client from first contact → intake → a session → notes → preparing for the next
    session. Tool/device at each step?
5T. **Session notes:** where do they live, what format, and *when* do you write them (during/after)?
6T. Before a session, how do you **remind yourself where you left off** with that client?
7T. Across dozens of clients, how do you avoid losing context / mixing people up?
8T. How do you handle cancellations, drop-off, and continuity if a colleague covers a session?

### D. Pain points & sizing (8 min)
For the 2–3 biggest frustrations they surfaced:
10. How **often** does that happen? (per day/week)
11. What does it **cost** you when it does — time, money, a lost patient, stress, a complaint?
12. **What have you done to fix it? Have you tried or bought anything?** What happened to that?
13. If you could wave a wand and fix exactly one thing about records/photos/patient memory, what?

### E. Vertical deep-dives (5 min — pick what's relevant)
- *Aesthetics:* marketing — how do new patients find you, monthly Instagram/ad spend, do before/after
  galleries drive bookings? Recalls/touch-ups — how tracked? Aftercare — do you give the patient anything?
- *Therapy:* confidentiality — any worry about digital/cloud notes? Pre-session prep value? Supervision/
  referrals/handoffs?

### F. Reflect the problem back — *before* the demo (3 min)
14. "Here's what I think I heard are your biggest problems: ___, ___, ___. Did I get it right? What's
    missing or wrong?" *(Validates you understood; catches the pain you mis-ranked. Still no product yet.)*

### G. Demo + reactions (15 min)
- Frame: **"Let me show you what we're building. Be brutal — tell me where it's wrong, useless, or won't
  fit your day."**
- Demo the flows that map to *their stated* pains (don't tour features they didn't mention):
  capture → auto-organize by patient → before/after gallery → fast Persian search → patient report +
  aftercare. *(Pro:* dictation → structured visit report, patient memory/recall, patient Q&A.)
- **Watch and note:** where they lean in / say "can it do X?", where they're confused, where they say
  "yes but…", which feature they reach for first, which they ignore.
- After:
15. Which part would you use **tomorrow**? Which is **useless** to you?
16. What's **missing** before you'd switch from what you use now?
17. Would your **assistant/reception** actually use the capture/intake part? What would stop them?
18. On a scale, how painful is switching from your current setup — and what makes it painful (data,
    retraining, habit)?

### H. Willingness to pay (8 min — value-anchored, after the demo)
First the substitute anchors, then Van Westendorp, then the real number.
19. What do you pay today for clinic/booking/records software (monthly)?
20. What does an assistant who handles records earn monthly here?
Show the one-line Basic and Pro (therapy: the single plan) descriptions, and for each ask:
21. At what monthly price is it **so cheap you'd doubt its quality**?
22. At what price is it **a bargain**?
23. At what price does it get **expensive but still worth considering**?
24. At what price is it **too expensive to consider**?
25. Real number: *"Basic [≈toman of $15], Pro [≈toman of $30] per clinic, plus a fee per extra doctor."*
    Gut reaction — high, fair, cheap?
26. Does **paying per doctor** feel fair, or do you expect one flat clinic price?

### I. Adoption, trust & buying (5 min — the deal-breakers)
27. **Devices/OS** in the clinic (iPad? Android/iPhone? Windows desktop?), and is the internet reliable?
    Who's comfortable with apps, who isn't?
28. **Data & privacy:** would storing patient photos/notes in the cloud worry you or your patients? Do you
    prefer data kept locally? Any patient-consent issues with that?
29. **Payment reality:** how do you pay for software today (rial gateway, card)? Would it be a problem if
    payment had to go through a particular method?
30. If you said no to this, what would the **reason** be? What single change would turn it into a yes?
31. Who signs off, what's the budget, and would you prefer monthly or yearly?

### J. Close & commitment (3 min)
32. If it were available today, would you sign up — at what price, monthly/yearly?
33. **Would you join a paid pilot next month?** *(The real signal. Try to schedule the next step now.)*
34. Who else — 2 peers in another clinic — should we talk to?

## Part 3 — What we added beyond pain + WTP, and why (the "did we miss anything" guideline)

You asked me to predict the important things to interview. Beyond current-workflow/pain/demo/WTP, these
five areas decide whether a clinical tool actually lands — most pre-PMF teams skip them and pay for it:

1. **Adoption & device reality (Sec I-27).** Clinical software dies on the staff who won't use it. If the
   clinic is on Windows desktops or the assistant resists apps, capture-first is dead on arrival regardless
   of value. *Test the weakest user, not the enthusiastic owner.*
2. **Data / privacy / trust (Sec I-28).** Patient photos + notes are sensitive; trusting a startup's cloud
   is a real barrier, and patient consent for photos is a live issue in aesthetics. This can block the sale
   even when the value is obvious.
3. **Payment & operational reality in Iran (Sec I-29).** A product they love but *can't pay for* (gateway/
   sanctions friction) is a no. Surface it before it surprises you.
4. **Switching cost (Sec G-18).** "Better than Apple Notes" still loses if migrating years of records and
   retraining staff feels heavy. Quantify the switching pain; it shapes onboarding and pricing.
5. **The buyer vs. the user (Sec B-3, I-31).** In multi-doctor clinics the owner buys but doctors/reception
   use. If you only interview owners, you'll build for the wrong person. Deliberately capture both voices.

Two more worth a light touch if time allows: **decision/seasonality** (do they buy tools at certain
times?) and **competitive history** (what local clinic software they've tried and abandoned, and *why* —
the abandonment reason is gold).

## Part 5 — Hypotheses we're testing (so the interviewer knows the "so what")

Tag each interview's capture sheet with which of these it **supported** or **killed**:

| # | Hypothesis | Mainly tested by |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | Apple Notes / camera roll / WhatsApp is the real incumbent, and it genuinely hurts | C, D |
| H2 | Finding a returning patient's history / "what did I use last time" is slow & error-prone | C6–C7 |
| H3 | Before/after photo chaos is a top, frequent pain in aesthetics | C5, E |
| H4 | Galleries drive marketing/bookings → acquisition value (aesthetics) | E |
| H5 | Persian name search/matching is a specific, felt pain | C6 |
| H6 | Capture-first / never-block fits the real clinical flow, incl. assistants | C, G17 |
| H7 | The Pro AI layer (structured report, memory, recall) is worth a real upcharge | G, H |
| H8 | A between-visit patient-question deluge exists and is unmanaged (→ Pro Q&A) | C8 |
| H9 | Patient-facing report + aftercare is wanted (Basic value) | E, G15 |
| H10 | Therapy's value floor is the AI continuity/pre-session brief, not raw notes | 6T, 7T, G |
| H11 | WTP supports ≥ $15 Basic / $30 Pro per clinic + per-seat (Tehran) | H |
| H12 | Cloud-data trust and payment friction are surmountable, not deal-breakers | I |

A hypothesis is **validated** only by past behavior / spend / a pilot commitment — not by "that sounds
useful."
